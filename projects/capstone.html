Capstone Computing Project I and II

DareDivers

Overview

DareDivers is a 2D multiplayer survival game developed in Unity using C#. Inspired by Lethal Company, the game challenges players to embark on underwater expeditions to recover artifacts while surviving oxygen depletion, hostile sea creatures, and environmental hazards. This project was developed collaboratively by our entire team, with each member contributing across various aspects of the game's development. Rather than assigning rigid roles, we worked closely together on design decisions, programming tasks, asset integration, and testing. Throughout the project, I often took initiative in organizing development efforts—helping coordinate team communication, maintaining structured documentation, and managing our workflow through GitHub and Discord to keep us on track with milestone goals.

Background and Purpose

Our capstone course is designed to bridge the gap between academic theory and practical software development. The project was an opportunity to apply the following learning outcomes in a collaborative, real-world scenario:

  • Utilize new technologies and development tools, such as Unity, GitHub, and testing frameworks.
  • Conduct quality assurance and testing to improve software reliability.
  • Design and implement a complete computer-based system using modern development methodologies.
  • Work effectively in a team, contributing meaningfully to group efforts.
  • Communicate ideas and progress clearly, both verbally and in writing, to peers and stakeholders.

We chose to develop a 2D game because of its unique blend of challenges across multiple domains—real-time systems, networked gameplay, UI/UX, and asset integration. Game development provided a comprehensive test of our technical abilities, creativity, and collaboration.

Development Challenges

Team-Based Development in Unity

One of our primary challenges was adjusting to collaborative development within Unity. Unity is not inherently optimized for team environments, and without proper configuration, issues with asset linking and file versioning can quickly arise. We resolved this by standardizing Unity versions across the team and clearly defining development responsibilities to avoid overlapping edits—particularly in scene files, which are notoriously difficult to merge.

Version Control and Meta File Management

A major technical hurdle we encountered involved Unity’s .meta files, which are crucial for maintaining links between assets. Due to misconfigured .gitignore settings, these files weren’t being tracked properly, leading to broken references and frequent confusion. It wasn’t until several months into development that we fully diagnosed the issue. Once corrected, our workflow became significantly more stable, and asset loss or scene corruption drastically decreased.

Managing Merge Conflicts and Task Coordination

Because Unity scenes are not human-readable and are prone to merge conflicts, we approached branching and merging with caution. Tasks were clearly divided and tracked using GitHub Issues, while milestones and documentation were updated via the repository’s Wiki. We took a preventative approach to conflict resolution by communicating regularly about scene changes and using Discord to coordinate merges.

Streamlined Communication

Effective communication was central to our success. I organized our Discord server with specialized channels for announcements, development updates, GitHub alerts, bug tracking, and more. This allowed us to minimize confusion and ensure that important updates—such as when not to pull from a branch—were clearly communicated. I also kept track of team meetings and checked in regularly with teammates to identify blockers, provide support, and foster collaboration. When one team member encountered a persistent issue with their development environment, I helped them resolve it by walking them through recloning the correct repository and restoring their local setup.

Conclusion

The DareDivers capstone project was more than just a game—it was a comprehensive exercise in modern software development. It challenged us to think critically, work collaboratively, and apply both technical and interpersonal skills in a real-world context. Through the process, I grew not only as a developer but also as a teammate, communicator, and problem-solver. This experience has prepared me to confidently contribute to complex projects and adapt to the ever-evolving demands of the software industry.
