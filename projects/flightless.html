Flightless

MART 581D: Fall 2024

Overview

Flightless is a narrative-driven 2.5D platformer developed as a solo project for MART 581D. The game follows a pigeon with an injured wing navigating city rooftops in search of purpose, belonging, and courage. The project was designed to explore storytelling through gameplay mechanics, with a focus on environmental navigation, emotional resonance, and visual narrative. A short demo was published on Itch.io for peer playtesting and feedback.

Background and Purpose

The purpose of this project was to apply narrative design principles to an interactive digital experience. As part of MART 581D, I sought to blend story, environment, and mechanics into a cohesive game prototype. Inspired by themes of resilience and vulnerability, Flightless aimed to express emotional depth through minimalist gameplay, emphasizing exploration over combat. A 20-page Game Design Document was created to structure the game's development, detailing character arcs, level progression, control schemes, and technical considerations. This project also emphasized iterative design, requiring multiple rounds of testing, critique, and revision. My objective was not only to produce a working prototype but to demonstrate growth in systems thinking, storytelling, and user-centered design.

Development Challenges

Time Management & Focus

One of the biggest hurdles was managing my time effectively. I often hyper-focused on specific parts of the project—spending hours refining one mechanic or scene—while neglecting other coursework or project areas. This imbalance led to many sleepless nights and unnecessary burnout. I learned that dedicating too much energy to a single task can actually stall overall progress.

Absence of Version Control

Without using version control tools like Git or GitHub, project files were stored only locally. This presented risks related to file loss and hindered the ability to backtrack or manage iterations effectively. I’ve since recognized the value of implementing version control even in solo projects for better organization, collaboration potential, and long-term preservation.

Overambition

Initially, the vision for Flightless was expansive, featuring multiple levels, deep story arcs, and highly polished gameplay. However, the scope proved too large to complete within a single semester. As deadlines approached, I had to scale back significantly and refocus the project on core mechanics and narrative beats. This was a valuable lesson in scoping realistically and planning project phases in alignment with available time and resources.

Lack of Development Documentation

In addition to the absence of version control, I struggled with maintaining consistent development notes. Without a structured record of completed tasks, pending features, or known issues, it was difficult to pick up where I left off after breaks. I’ve learned that documenting progress and creating simple checklists or dev logs can significantly improve focus, continuity, and decision-making throughout development.

Incomplete but Ongoing

Although the game remains unfinished, it represents a strong foundation for continued work. I view the current build as a working prototype—one that successfully demonstrates core gameplay and narrative themes. I plan to further develop and refine Flightless beyond the course timeline, incorporating additional levels, polish, and expanded storytelling elements.

Conclusion

Developing Flightless was a deeply educational and rewarding experience. It sharpened my skills in game design, narrative development, Unity programming, and project management, while also teaching me critical lessons about scope, time management, and solo development workflows. While the project is still in progress, it marks a meaningful step forward in my journey as a game developer, and I am excited to continue its development in the future.
